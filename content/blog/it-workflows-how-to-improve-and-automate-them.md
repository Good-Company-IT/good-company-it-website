Before investing in another software tool, take a closer look at how work already gets done. Inefficient IT workflows can slow down operations, create unnecessary manual work, and increase the risk of errors. The best place to start is not necessarily the most complicated process—it is the one where a practical improvement can make a measurable difference.

For growing businesses, improving workflows means identifying where time is lost, understanding why bottlenecks happen, and choosing technology that solves the actual problem.

## What Are IT Workflows?

IT workflows are the sequences of tasks, decisions, and technology systems used to complete recurring business or IT activities. They can include onboarding employees, managing access permissions, processing service requests, maintaining software, and backing up business data.

A well-designed workflow makes responsibilities clear, reduces unnecessary steps, and helps work move consistently from one stage to the next.

## 5 IT Workflows Your Business Should Consider Automating

### 1\. Employee onboarding and offboarding

Creating accounts, assigning permissions, configuring devices, and removing access when someone leaves can involve several people and systems. Standardizing these steps and automating appropriate tasks helps reduce delays and the risk of forgotten permissions.

### 2\. Repetitive IT support requests

Password resets, software access requests, and routine troubleshooting can consume time that your team could spend on more complex issues. A structured service desk, self-service options, and automated ticket routing can make these requests easier to manage.

### 3\. Software updates and routine maintenance

Where tools support it, automated patch management and maintenance schedules can help teams apply updates consistently. Exceptions and failed updates still need monitoring, especially for systems that support critical business operations.

### 4\. Data backups and alerts

Automated backups and system alerts can reduce reliance on manual checks. However, automation alone does not guarantee recoverability. Schedule restoration tests to verify that important data can be recovered when needed.

### 5\. Reporting and repetitive administrative tasks

If employees repeatedly collect information from different systems to prepare reports, approve requests, or update records, workflow automation may reduce duplicate work. Start with stable, rules-based processes and ensure the resulting data remains accurate.

## How to Decide What to Automate First

Not every repetitive task is a good automation candidate. Before selecting a tool, evaluate each process against four criteria:

*   Frequency: How often does the task occur?
*   Time and effort: How much staff time does it consume?
*   Error and risk: What happens if a step is missed or completed incorrectly?
*   Feasibility: Can the process be standardized, and do your existing systems support automation?

Choose a process that occurs frequently, has clear steps, and offers a meaningful benefit without requiring a complex implementation. Document the current workflow, establish a baseline, and measure the results after making changes.

## Common Automation Mistakes to Avoid

Automating a poorly designed process can make inefficiencies happen faster. Before implementation, remove unnecessary steps and clarify who owns each decision.

Avoid introducing tools without checking integration requirements, access controls, data privacy, and ongoing maintenance. Keep human review where judgment, exceptions, or sensitive decisions require it. For AI-enabled workflows, define what information the tool can access and who verifies its output.
